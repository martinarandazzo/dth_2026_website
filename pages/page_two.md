## What are the disadvantages of open source software?

### 1. Lack of formal leadership and accountability
Unlike proprietary software, where the company that sells the program is legally responsible for supporting it under a service contract, open source software has no warranty or liability. Because volunteer contributors share their work for free, licenses protect them if the program crashes or causes data loss. This creates a major liability for users as there will be no official hotlines nor are the contributors mandated to fix the issue immediately.
### 2. Security risks 
The source code being public can be a double-edged sword, while contributors could look for issues to fix, someone else might look for holes to maliciously exploit, like in the case of XZ utils where contributor Jia Tan exploited trust of its one and only creator and maintainer Lasse Collin to hide a malicious code inside the project through a backdoor.
### 3. Hidden costs
Having no upfront paywall does not always mean something is completely free.
To cover costs some open source projects might rely on targeted ads, tracking cookies or built-in usage telemetry. In some other cases one might have to pay to use additional features of the program’s site. Thus, one may unknowingly pay with their personal data.
### 4. Project abandonment and volunteer burnout
Since most of the work on open source software projects is voluntary, meaning unpaid, sometimes the people working on them might get very busy with their lives, jobs or other projects or might even lose interest in the project itself. Thus, a lot of projects might get abandoned or left alone for long periods of time. This might also lead ill-intentioned people to make use of possible holes or bugs and insert malwares.
